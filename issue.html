🌱 Personal finance: tools, ideas & innovation
Sainsbury's dips back into loans and savings
Sainsbury's Money has relaunched into personal loans and is preparing an instant-access savings account for October, powered by NatWest's embedded finance platform, Boxed. Announced on 28 September, loans range from £1,000 to £35,000, with Nectar card holders offered preferential rates from 6.3% APR against 6.6% for non-members. The planned savings account promises no penalty for dipping in or out. The exact savings rate for Nectar members has not yet been published (see Flagged) — NatWest describes it only as "even better" ahead of launch. Worth knowing if you already shop with Nectar and fancy comparing the member rate against your current account.
natwestgroup.com — NatWest Group press release, 28 Sep 2026
A free AI financial adviser launches
Fintech veteran Bill Harris, who previously ran PayPal and Intuit, launched Evergreen.ai on 22 September — a free AI-powered financial guidance platform. It pairs a chatbot interface with a "deterministic calculation engine," so its retirement, mortgage and tax projections are checkable rather than simply plausible-sounding text. Banking data is processed via Plaid rather than fed straight to the AI, and beta sign-ups keep free access until January 2028. With 73% of American adults not using a financial adviser, tools like this are worth exploring for a second opinion on your own numbers — though any AI output is a starting point for your own checking, not the final word.
fintech.global, 22 Sep 2026
Why only 7% of women feel confident investing
A KashKick survey of 25,000 US adults, published 30 September, found that just 7% of women feel very or extremely comfortable investing, whatever their income. 65% of women hold no investments at all, against 49% of men — and even among women earning six figures, over a third have none, more than double the rate for men on the same pay. The researchers frame this as a confidence and access gap rather than purely a money one: workplace pension schemes, where the first step is automatic, see women save more than men. Encouragingly, 28% of women said they'd be open to trying automated savings or interest-earning tools — often the simplest way in.
GlobeNewswire — KashKick survey, 30 Sep 2026

Bond markets flash a warning as yields hit a 24-year high
The US 10-year Treasury yield climbed to around 5.33% this week, its highest since 2002, as investors recalculated how much further the Federal Reserve may need to raise rates. The move follows September's quarter-point hike, with Fed governors Michael Barr and Lisa Cook signalling that inflation, near 3.8%, remains well above the 2% target — markets are now pricing a real chance of a further rise before year-end. Three forces are pushing yields up together: persistent inflation, heavy government borrowing for a growing deficit, and surging capital demand from the AI build-out, which some analysts say is crowding other borrowers out. This matters beyond Wall Street: Treasury yields set the benchmark for mortgage, car-loan and corporate borrowing costs economy-wide, and higher yields tend to weigh on equity valuations, particularly for growth and technology shares priced on distant future earnings.
Yahoo Finance, 1 Oct 2026
Oil surges as Middle East tensions escalate, adding fuel to the inflation fire
Oil prices jumped sharply this week — Brent crude rising over 4% above $102 a barrel and US benchmark WTI gaining over 2.7% to around $93 — after reports the United States is sending a third aircraft carrier and up to 10,000 additional troops to the Middle East, alongside combative rhetoric towards Iran. Chinese refiners reportedly suspended fuel exports beyond Hong Kong and Macau, citing domestic supply concerns, compounding existing global diesel shortages following earlier damage to Gulf infrastructure and Russian export restrictions. The Strait of Hormuz, a critical oil-shipping route, has remained effectively disrupted since the US and Israel's joint military action against Iran earlier this year. For investors, the renewed spike matters because energy costs feed directly into inflation readings, reinforcing the case for central banks to hold rates higher for longer; for households, it raises the prospect of steeper fuel and transport costs just as borrowing costs climb too.
Euronext Live, 1 Oct 2026

💎 The company to follow — Micron Technology
Micron posted record fiscal fourth-quarter results on 1 October: revenue of $54.23bn and non-GAAP earnings of $33.42 a share, comfortably ahead of forecasts. The driver is AI-related demand — data-centre SSD revenue approached $10bn, roughly ten times a year ago, while tight DRAM and NAND supply pushed prices higher across the board. Management now expects 2027 to be "another record year," with memory markets staying supply-constrained. The results rippled outward, lifting chipmakers Nvidia, Intel and Broadcom, since Micron's memory chips underpin much of the AI server build-out. Worth understanding as a bellwether for how AI infrastructure spending moves through supply chains.
Yahoo Finance, 1 Oct 2026
💎 One to be careful of — J.B. Hunt Transport Services
J.B. Hunt, one of America's largest trucking and logistics groups, saw shares slide around 13% in September after warning third-quarter earnings per share could fall 5–10% below the second quarter's $1.91. Management pointed to record diesel prices — $6.31 a gallon nationally — alongside roughly $25m in extra driver recruitment and retention costs. The mismatch is structural: 96% of operating income comes from intermodal and dedicated contracts that reprice slowly, leaving the firm unable to pass rising costs to customers quickly. Executives called the pressure cyclical, but the episode is a clear reminder of how fuel and labour costs can squeeze even well-run logistics businesses. Caution, not a verdict — worth watching closely before drawing conclusions.
AskTraders, Sep 2026
💎 Innovation in investment — Robinhood's weekend trading and AI "Agents"
Robinhood used its Hood Summit on 30 September to unveil weekend stock trading, pending regulatory approval, via an institutional-grade system called Bruce ATS, extending its existing 24-hour weekday market. Alongside this, "Robinhood Agents" — AI tools built into the app — already have more than 150,000 users executing around 30 million actions daily, with access to models including OpenAI's GPT-Luna, free until year-end. The firm is also rolling out premium market-data subscriptions, leveraged crypto perpetual futures, and fee-free earnings prediction markets. Together, these moves show retail platforms blurring the line between investing, trading and always-on automation — a trend worth following, though higher-risk features such as leveraged crypto deserve particular caution.
Benzinga, 30 Sep 2026
💎 A new-CEO adventure that will make a difference — John Ternus, Apple
Apple's leadership transition is entering its next chapter. John Ternus, a 25-year Apple veteran and former hardware engineering chief, took over as chief executive on 1 September after Tim Cook's 15-year tenure ended; Cook remains executive chairman. Bloomberg reported on 29 September that Ternus is weighing a significant overhaul — moving away from Apple's traditional spring-software, autumn-hardware launch rhythm towards more frequent releases, and flattening management by cutting director-level roles, with cuts already touching Siri, Vision Pro and AI software teams. Apple shares dipped modestly on the report. The question worth understanding for long-term Apple watchers: whether a leaner, engineering-led Apple can close the AI gap with rivals faster than the old structure allowed.
Yahoo Finance, citing Bloomberg, 29 Sep 2026
🔮 Investment: the bigger picture
Why forecasters are growing more upbeat on Q4 — but not uniformly
Several analysts are nudging their outlooks for the final quarter of 2026 higher, pointing to an unusually strong earnings season as the main driver. FXStreet's preview of third-quarter reporting puts S&P 500 year-on-year earnings growth near 24% — an eighth consecutive quarter of double-digit growth — with 14 of 16 sectors forecast to grow, led by technology (up almost 42%), aerospace and energy. Analysts note that estimates for Q4 2026 and even full-year 2027 "have also been trending higher lately." Separately, cooler-than-expected August inflation data (core PCE at 3.4% against a 3.7% forecast) gave some strategists room to argue the Fed could ease its pace of rate moves. Northlight Asset Management's Chris Zaccarelli suggested markets may need a strong earnings season and clarity past the US midterm elections before breaking out of their current trading range. Worth understanding: forecasts diverge — some houses stress earnings durability, others flag stretched valuations and election-related uncertainty.
FXStreet, 28 Sep 2026  ·  TheStreet, 30 Sep 2026
Sibos 2026: banks road-test AI agents, but keep humans "holding the keys"
At Sibos 2026, the banking industry's flagship global conference held in Miami from 28 September to 1 October, the dominant theme was how quickly agentic AI is moving from pilot projects into live banking operations — and how institutions are trying to govern that shift. Reporting from the conference floor described banks and payment networks discussing AI agents that can already negotiate account terms on a customer's behalf, with Mastercard's Johan Gerber citing an agent opening with: "I'm representing your customer X, and I'm here to renegotiate my credit card terms with you." Executives from Société Générale, Standard Chartered, State Street and Nasdaq debated trust, identity verification and liability frameworks — concerns panellists said need resolving industry-wide before external regulators step in. McKinsey's Uzayr Jeenah was reported as projecting that machine-speed, largely autonomous operations could become standard within two to three years, though cross-platform interoperability and accountability remain unresolved. The consistent message: banks want AI handling routine execution, while people keep the judgement calls — for now.
FinTech Futures, 1 Oct 2026
✨ Girl in Finance Fun Fact Friday
The 400-year-old IOU that's still being paid
Tucked inside the New York Stock Exchange is a soggy bit of Dutch history: a bond issued in January 1624 by a water authority near Utrecht, raised to repair a dike after a flood. It's a "perpetual bond" — the principal never gets repaid, but the interest still does. Every single year, the present-day Dutch water board dutifully posts a cheque for €13.61. Not glamorous, but wonderfully persistent proof that debt, structured cleverly enough, can outlive empires, currencies, and quite possibly you.
NPR, 17 Nov 2023
