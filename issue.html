🌱 Personal finance: tools, ideas & innovation
X launches its own digital wallet
X Money began rolling out to Premium subscribers in the US on 29 July, built with Cross River Bank and Visa. It bundles peer-to-peer transfers, bill payments and a Visa debit card with no foreign transaction fees, plus up to 6% APY on deposits and early paycheck access. Deposits carry FDIC pass-through cover up to $250,000 per bank. It's a clear sign "everything apps" are pushing further into everyday banking — worth watching as social platforms edge into money management. FinTech Futures
A record seed round for an AI wealth adviser
São Paulo startup Decade announced an $85 million seed round on 4 August — reportedly the largest seed round ever for a Latin American startup — led by Greenoaks, with Benchmark and Diffusion also investing. Founded by former Nubank global CTO Vitor Olivier, Decade pairs clients with a human adviser plus a proprietary AI tracking spending, portfolios and goals. Currently invite-only and aimed at affluent individuals in Brazil, it's a signal of where AI-assisted wealth advice is heading. BusinessWire
Ask your brokerage account a question — in plain English
Trading platform Webull announced on 4 August native connectors linking brokerage accounts to ChatGPT, Claude and Grok, so users can ask natural-language questions like "what's in my portfolio?" and get account-specific answers on balances, positions and market data. Group President Anthony Denier said "AI is rapidly becoming a preferred interface for investors to interact with their brokerage account." An early sign of general chat tools becoming a front door to investing apps for newer investors — worth understanding, not a recommendation to use any tool. PR Newswire

The Fed is now weighing a hike, not a cut, as jobs data cools
The Federal Reserve, now under chair Kevin Warsh, held rates steady at its 29 July meeting — but three regional presidents dissented in favour of an immediate hike, the most hawkish split since September 2016. Tech Times Markets are now pricing roughly a 60% chance of a quarter-point hike at the mid-September meeting, and the 10-year Treasury yield has climbed toward 4.7%, up from 4.4% in late June. Forbes The backdrop: June payrolls grew just 57,000 with unemployment at 4.2%, CNBC and ADP data released 6 August showed July private hiring at only 44,000 — the weakest since January. Tech Times Persistently high energy prices and tariff-driven inflation are cited as reasons the Fed could tighten even as hiring cools — worth understanding if you carry variable-rate debt. $100bn in tariff refunds are flowing — but the story isn't over
The Supreme Court ruled 6-3 that Trump's sweeping "reciprocal" tariffs, imposed under emergency powers, exceeded his authority. Kiplinger Since then, US Customs has repaid businesses through a new refund platform — $100bn of the $166bn owed, as of 5 August. Apple is receiving about $2.2bn and says it will reinvest into US manufacturing; Amazon's $600m refund is largely being absorbed, while Walmart and Costco have pledged price cuts. ABC News The Tax Foundation estimated the now-void tariffs had cost a typical household roughly $700 a year — though the refunds are landing with companies, not directly with shoppers. Crucially, the ruling hasn't ended tariffs: duties imposed under other legal routes remain, including a fresh 50% tariff on Canadian goods introduced in late July, alongside existing steel, aluminium and China-linked duties. Kiplinger

The company to follow: Palantir Technologies
Palantir posted a standout quarter this week: Q2 revenue of $1.94bn, up 93% year-on-year, US commercial revenue up 149%, and net income of $1.06bn versus $326.7m a year earlier. It raised its full-year revenue forecast to $8.15–8.16bn, well above its prior range, and shares jumped as much as 12% on the news — helping drive the S&P 500 and Dow to record closes on 4 August. CEO Alex Karp called the results "otherworldly." A useful case study in how a strong earnings beat plus raised guidance can move a stock and the broader market — educational context, not a recommendation. Yahoo Finance, TheStreet
One to be careful of: Zillow Group
Zillow's shares fell sharply on 6 August after Q2 results showed a $4m net loss, driven by $36m in restructuring costs, and confirmation of over 500 job cuts. Q3 revenue guidance of $745–760m missed the $772.3m analysts expected. Several analysts cut price targets in response — DA Davidson from $60 to $50, Susquehanna from $50 to $38, Cantor Fitzgerald from $34 to $32 — though most kept positive overall ratings. A reminder that even companies beating some earnings metrics can face real headwinds elsewhere — worth understanding the full picture, not a recommendation either way. Daily Political
Innovation in investment: tokenised US stocks go retail
On 4 August, blockchain brokerage Dinari launched a platform letting eligible US investors buy tokenised versions of 724 US stocks — including every S&P 500 company — as blockchain-based shares, funded via stablecoin through self-custody wallets. Underlying shares are held 1:1 in regulated brokerage accounts, and holders keep voting rights and dividends. FINRA had not previously allowed broker-dealers to accept stablecoins for stock purchases, making this a genuine regulatory first, reviewed by the SEC. An early, real-world example of "tokenisation" moving from concept to product — worth understanding as the technology develops, not an endorsement. CoinDesk
A new-CEO adventure that will make a difference: Aymeric Le Clere
On 4 August, Bernard Arnault's holding company Financière Agache — the vehicle through which he controls his majority stake in LVMH — named Aymeric Le Clere as its new CEO, replacing Florian Ollivier. Le Clere is a former banker who spent roughly seven years at Agache and served as CFO of Christian Dior SE, with earlier experience in equities at Natixis. The appointment lands amid intensifying scrutiny of succession planning at the world's largest luxury group, as LVMH only recently returned to sales growth after two soft years. Worth watching for anyone interested in how leadership choices ripple through one of the world's most influential luxury and investment empires. FashionNetwork (see Flagged)

How banks are evolving: the charter gold rush
Under Comptroller Jonathan Gould, the US Office of the Comptroller of the Currency is fast-tracking national bank charters for fintech and digital-asset firms, aiming to clear applications within 120 days. American Banker reports roughly two dozen neobanks, crypto firms, lenders and payments companies have applied for or received charters in 2026. American Banker Confirmed approvals include Circle, Upstart Bank and Coinbase's national trust charter; on 4 August, Augustus National Bank — an AI/stablecoin clearing bank — won FDIC deposit insurance approval, with BitGo, Fidelity Digital Assets, Paxos and Morgan Stanley Digital Trust among those pending. Law firm Davis Wright Tremaine's analysts argue tokenised deposits and securities will become "a practical necessity" for banks within years; community banking trade groups have publicly opposed some approvals. Why your piggy bank is shaped like a pig
It's not by design — well, actually, it kind of is. In medieval Europe, people stored coins in jars made from a cheap orange clay called "pygg." Ask a potter for a "pygg jar" often enough, and centuries later the word had drifted from clay type to farm animal — so 19th-century craftsmen, taking the name literally, started sculpting the containers as actual pigs. Pure linguistic accident, pure delight. So next time you drop spare change into that ceramic snout, you're honouring a 600-year-old pun nobody meant to make. Federal Reserve Bank of New York
