🌱 Personal finance: tools, ideas & innovation
An AI coach gives your spending a cat-themed personality
Malaysia's AEON Bank has launched a free feature in its app called Financial Purr-Sona. An AI coach named Neko Sensei reads how you actually use money — saving, card spending, bill payments, QR payments — and sorts you into one of four "personas," from the disciplined Planner Pro Max to the easy-come-easy-go Boss Anti-Bazir. The persona shifts as habits change, and using more of the bank's features sharpens the read. A "Level-up" section then serves tailored budgeting tips, free of charge. It is a playful version of something most money apps already do quietly: turning transaction data into a nudge worth reading. Worth knowing about if gamified budgeting appeals to you. The Star, 27 Oct 2026
A buy-now-pay-later giant wants to become an actual bank
Sezzle, the buy-now-pay-later company, has applied to the US Office of the Comptroller of the Currency for a national bank charter, seeking to set up Sezzle Bank, N.A. with FDIC deposit insurance. If approved, it plans to offer chequing and savings accounts alongside its existing instalment credit, aimed at digitally native customers who tend to avoid traditional credit products. Nothing changes for customers yet — regulators typically take 12 to 18 months to review this kind of application — but it signals a wider shift: buy-now-pay-later firms moving from pure lending into everyday banking. Worth watching rather than acting on. Banking Dive · PYMNTS
HSBC launches a digital Hong Kong dollar inside its everyday payments app
HSBC has begun rolling out RedCoin, a new digital token pegged one-to-one to the Hong Kong dollar, through PayMe, its everyday payments app used by more than 3.3 million people. In practice it works like topping up a wallet: HK$1 buys one RedCoin, which can be sent to friends or spent at more than 100,000 participating merchants, using the app people already have on their phones. HSBC is offering transaction perks to encourage uptake, and says tokenised investment products — starting with a money-market fund accessible from HK$100 — are coming before year-end. A reminder that digital-money innovation is not confined to crypto apps: high-street banks are building it into apps millions already use. cryptometer.io, 23 Oct 2026

What happened: the Fed's split hold, in a week of Big Tech earnings
On Wednesday 28 October, the US Federal Reserve held its benchmark interest rate at 3.75%–4.00% for a second straight meeting — a decision that split its own rate-setting committee. The vote was 10 to 2, with two governors, Lisa Cook and Beth Hammack, preferring a further quarter-point rise rather than a pause. The Fed's statement cited resilient economic activity and elevated inflation as reasons for caution. Chair Kevin Warsh's press conference landed in the same week as a wave of Big Tech earnings, adding extra noise to trading. Wall Street extended its gains regardless: the S&P 500 closed at 7,743.98, a third consecutive daily rise, while the Dow and Nasdaq also edged higher. Bond markets were more cautious, with the 10-year Treasury yield climbing to around 5.03%. The Fed's next scheduled decision falls on 9 December. The Market Breakdown · Yahoo Finance
Why it matters: solid growth, sticky inflation, and a huge AI bill
This pause matters because it keeps borrowing costs on mortgages, credit cards and business loans at their highest level in years, even as some policymakers wanted them higher still. A 10–2 vote is an unusually public split for the Fed, pointing to more uncertainty ahead: markets were pricing roughly an 85% chance of a further quarter-point rise in December. The timing matters too — the decision landed the same week as earnings from Microsoft and Alphabet, which showed two different faces of the AI investment boom. Microsoft's Azure business grew 48% while trimming its spending plans, reassuring investors; Alphabet's cloud revenue more than doubled, yet heavy capital spending left it with negative free cash flow for the quarter, and its shares fell more than 10%. Together, the two stories reveal one tension running through the economy: solid growth, inflation that has not fully cooled, and enormous AI bills still being paid. The Market Breakdown

🔮 Investment: the bigger picture
The Fed held firm this week — but the banks say December is different
On Wednesday 28 October, the US Federal Reserve voted 10-2 to hold its benchmark interest rate at 3.75%–4.00%. Two policymakers, Governor Lisa Cook and Cleveland Fed President Beth Hammack, voted against the hold, preferring a further quarter-point increase, arguing inflation remains too high. What stands out is how aligned forecasters now are about what comes next. A Reuters poll of 100 economists, taken 16–20 October, found all 100 expect the Fed to raise rates again in December — a sharp shift from under half who expected that just a month earlier. Separately, a Reuters survey of 13 major brokerages — including Goldman Sachs, J.P. Morgan, Morgan Stanley, UBS, Barclays, Deutsche Bank and Citigroup — found 12 expect one December rise to 4.00%–4.25%, while BofA Global Research expects a larger move to 4.25%–4.50%. Worth following, not acting on: no single forecast is a certainty. Reuters via Capital.com · Reuters via 93.3 The Drive
Banks are locking the door on AI agents in your account
One of the more unusual banking stories this month: major US banks, including Morgan Stanley, Bank of America, JPMorgan Chase, Wells Fargo, Citi and Charles Schwab, have begun restricting third-party AI "agents" from logging into customer accounts, after detecting unvetted agents operating inside them, some acting without clear customer instruction. These agents often log in using a customer's own saved credentials, meaning banks cannot always tell whether a login is a human or a bot acting on their behalf. Morgan Stanley says it has blocked outside agents from its platforms; JPMorgan's Jamie Dimon says the bank "won't let just any outside agent" in; Citi permits only limited read-only access via approved providers; Bank of America's chief executive says the bank wants a human to authorise any money actually leaving an account. This sits alongside a broader shift: banks are investing heavily in their own in-house AI while building new defences against AI tools they do not control. IBTimes UK · Dealroom (Axios)
✨ Girl in Finance Fun Fact Friday
The money that never moves — Yap Island's giant stone cash
Picture a "coin" so large it takes several strong men and a thick pole just to shift it. On the Pacific island of Yap, traditional currency comes as rai stones — carved limestone discs up to twelve feet across and weighing as much as five tons. Here is the delightful part: when a rai stone changes hands, it usually does not move an inch. It stays propped outside a house or in the village square, while the community simply agrees who owns it now. No vault, no wallet, no app — just a very large rock and a shared memory of who holds the title. Rather charming, compared with a two-factor banking code. PCGS · Mental Floss
